# How IAM policies, roles and S3 bucket policies decide access

- **Date:** 2026-10-29
- **Topic:** Cloud Security
- **Source:** AWS Academy Learner Lab (Cloud Security), Lab 3.1 "Using Resource-Based Policies to Secure an S3 Bucket"
- **Time spent:** 60 min

## What I learned

- Identity-based policies are attached to a user, group or role and say what that identity can do. Resource-based policies, like S3 bucket policies, are attached to the resource and say who can use it.
- Access is allowed if either type of policy allows it and nothing explicitly denies it. My role had no permission for bucket2, but bucket2's own policy let the role upload to it.
- Assuming a role swaps your permissions for the role's, it doesn't add them together. As the role I could read bucket1 but lost IAM access I had as my normal user.
- A role's trust policy controls who is allowed to assume it. STS then hands out temporary credentials.
- Bucket-level and object-level S3 permissions are separate. I could create a bucket but not upload files into it.

## Why it matters for security

A bucket policy can grant access that never shows up when you only review IAM users and roles, so audits need to check both. An overly broad trust policy or bucket policy is an easy way for an attacker to move between identities or pull data out.
